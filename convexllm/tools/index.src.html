Overview

In brief C-Gate feeds audio to a language model as convex combinations of the model's own frozen text embeddings, so every audio vector lies inside the region the decoder was pretrained on.

Most speech and audio language models connect an audio encoder to a text-pretrained decoder through an MLP or a Q-Former. Those interfaces can place audio features anywhere in the decoder's input space, and the decoder has to learn to read them.

ConvexLLM uses a different interface, C-Gate. For each pooled audio position, C-Gate scores the decoder's vocabulary, keeps the 16 best rows of the frozen text-embedding table, and mixes them with non-negative weights that sum to one. The result is continuous, but it is always a convex combination of real text embeddings.

With a Whisper-large-v3 encoder and a Qwen3.5-9B decoder, ConvexLLM reaches 75.36 on an eleven-task VoiceBench aggregate, the highest among the open-weight models compared, and 62.50% on MMAU Test-mini. The largest gains are on spoken knowledge and reasoning. In a controlled comparison with a 2B decoder, C-Gate also scores higher than MLP and Q-Former interfaces on aggregate spoken-language performance, while its text-task performance stays close to the MLP interface.

Method

C-Gate

Let h_t be the pooled encoder output at position t and \mathbf{E} the decoder's text-embedding table. C-Gate projects the audio to a d-dimensional query, scores every vocabulary row against a learned key projection of \mathbf{E}, and keeps the top 16:

s_{t,v}=\frac{q_t^{\top}k_v}{\sqrt{d}\,\tau_r},\qquad q_t=W_q h_t,\quad k_v=W_k\mathbf{E}_v,\qquad S_t=\operatorname{top\text{-}16}_{v}\; s_{t,v}

The selected scores are normalised with a softmax at temperature \tau_m=0.7, and the output mixes the original embedding rows, not their projections:

\tilde{e}_t=\sum_{v\in S_t}\alpha_{t,v}\,\mathbf{E}_v,\qquad \alpha_{t,v}\ge 0,\quad \sum_{v\in S_t}\alpha_{t,v}=1

So \tilde{e}_t always lies in the convex hull of 16 vectors the decoder already knows. The key projection only decides which rows are used. The values passed to the decoder come straight from \mathbf{E}.

Training

Training runs in three stages that progressively unfreeze the model. The text-embedding table and the LM head stay frozen throughout, so the interface keeps pointing into a fixed vocabulary space.
